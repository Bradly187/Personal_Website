Network Intrusion Detection:
Classical vs. Quantum ML

Systematic comparison of PCA+SVM, deep learning (MLP), and quantum ML approaches for network intrusion detection on the NSL-KDD dataset. CSCI 459 | AI Enhanced Security, Texas A&M-RELLIS.

// Key findings
MLP (PyTorch) — Best Overall
79.9% binary accuracy, 0.791 F1 on the full 22,544-record KDDTest+ set, against a 43.1% dummy baseline under NSL-KDD's deliberate train/test distribution shift. Led every metric, including 5-class (78.2% acc, 0.746 weighted F1).
PCA + SVM (RBF) — Strong Classical Baseline
77.6% binary accuracy, 0.761 F1 with GridSearchCV-tuned hyperparameters (C=100, RBF), competitive with far less compute. Multi-class performance (74.0% acc) was limited by class imbalance in R2L and U2R.
Variational Quantum Classifier — Near-Classical on 6 Qubits
77.5% binary accuracy, 0.759 F1 with AngleEmbedding + StronglyEntanglingLayers on a PennyLane/Braket simulator (500/200 train/test subset) | within ~2 points of the full classical models at current qubit scale. A quantum-kernel QSVC was also run on IBM Quantum Heron R2 (ibm_fez).
Quantum Transfer Learning | Honest Negative Result
A 4-qubit transfer-learning head (real_amplitudes, reps=2) failed to learn a decision boundary | 43% accuracy, F1 = 0 after ~874s of training. Reported as-is: documenting what doesn't work at current qubit counts is part of the comparison.

Class Imbalance

R2L and U2R classes are severely underrepresented in training data, creating significant class imbalance challenges that suppressed multi-class F1 scores across all models.

Distribution Shift

The test set has a different class distribution than training, simulating real-world distribution shift posing a deliberate challenge in the NSL-KDD benchmark.
